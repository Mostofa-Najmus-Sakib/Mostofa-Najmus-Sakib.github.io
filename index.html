About Me

Building Intelligent AI Systems, From Research to Deployment

Generative AI · Agentic systems · Enterprise RAG · Adversarial robustness

I am an AI/ML engineer and Ph.D. candidate in Computing, with a Computer Science emphasis, at Boise State University. My focus is generative AI, tool-using agents, retrieval-augmented generation, and adversarial robustness. I work across the AI development lifecycle—from data pipelines and model experimentation to agent orchestration, APIs, evaluation, and deployment.

My recent engineering work includes an enterprise agentic RAG system for retrieving, analyzing, and comparing 15,000+ agricultural product labels. I combined LangGraph and LangChain orchestration with multi-source retrieval, LLM-based reranking, and MCP tools connecting agents to structured data and business systems. Label-comparison workflows, LangSmith evaluation and observability, and guardrails supported recommendations grounded in active ingredients, application constraints, location, and inventory.

Beyond LLM orchestration, I have developed Azure and Databricks pipelines for more than one million social-media interactions and applied sentiment and stance analysis to autonomous-vehicle public opinion. My software engineering experience includes Python APIs, cloud-hosted document automation, backend database integration, and Docker/Kubernetes deployment. I work with domain experts to turn business requirements into AI workflows and deliver iteratively through Agile development.

My PhD research at Boise State University's AI-based Security Lab focuses on human-centered AI robustness, adversarial NLP, and information credibility. I develop LLM-crafted credibility-drift attacks that manipulate linguistic, stylistic, and source-credibility cues to influence perceived news believability while preserving semantic similarity. My work includes developing LLM-based proxy models (ICL and COLA), evaluating adversarial attack effectiveness, investigating offline reinforcement learning-based defenses, and conducting human-subject studies to assess how AI-generated transformations affect credibility judgments and reasoning. I also develop graph neural network models integrating human believability signals to improve information reliability assessment. My broader research spans adversarial robustness, human-centered AI, and graph learning, with publications at WSDM, CSCW, ICWSM, and ASONAM.

Research artifact · WSDM 2026

Credibility Drift Attacks

Research on LLM-crafted, meaning-preserving news transformations that can change how believable a news article appears. The work studies five drift subtypes across ten scenarios and evaluates attacks with proxy credibility classifiers.

Research focus

Adversarial NLP, linguistic manipulation, and robustness evaluation of news-believability models.

The public repository provides the paper and research documentation.

Research dataset · CSCW 2025

News Believability

A research dataset accompanying Understanding News Consumers’ Perceptions of Believability: A Study of Real and Fake News.

From perception to modeling

The study combines 14,535 user comments, 188 news articles, and believability judgments from 611 participants. Believability-aware graph modeling improved classification accuracy from approximately 80% to 87%. The public repository provides news data and believability scores supporting the study.
